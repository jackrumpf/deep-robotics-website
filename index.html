FARP-S autonomous servicing station

Autonomous ground infrastructure for unmanned fleets.

FARP-S lands, inspects and services small uncrewed aircraft at remote and forward sites, then clears them for the next mission. Nobody needs to be standing at the landing point.

Autonomous vehicles still depend on human logistics.

A small rotary-wing aircraft flies for under an hour. Then it has to be recovered, inspected, recharged and prepared before it can go back up, and today almost all of that is done by hand.

  • Every additional uncrewed vehicle adds to the ground-support burden.
  • Manual servicing limits turnaround and persistent operations.
  • Different platforms need different ground equipment and procedures.

One turnaround, start to finish.

  1. Detect

    The mast sensor picks up the returning aircraft and confirms the airspace is clear.

  2. Land

    The aircraft sets down anywhere inside the capture ring.

  3. Localize

    Cameras and deck markers fix its exact position and heading.

  4. Fit the right tool

    If this airframe needs a different toolhead, the arm swaps it from the rack.

  5. Inspect

    A camera sweeps the props, booms and landing gear for damage.

  6. Restore power

    The arm makes the charging connection hands-free, or exchanges the pack.

  7. Verify and release

    Service is confirmed complete and the bay is clear before the aircraft leaves.

One station, any airframe.

Most docks are built around a cradle cut to fit one aircraft. Change the aircraft and you need a new dock.

FARP-S solves alignment with vision instead of hardware, and gives each arm a tool changer that draws universal toolheads from an onboard rack. Supporting another aircraft means adding a set of tools, not buying another station.

A fixed cradle

Mechanical guides centre one airframe. Precise, and permanently tied to that aircraft's geometry.

Vision and toolheads

A wrist camera closes the loop on the final approach, and the rack carries the right tool for each aircraft.

Built to be built.

The station is designed around catalogue parts and simple fabrication, so it can be built, changed and fielded without tooling or long-lead custom work.

  • FrameBolted T-slot aluminium extrusion
  • ManipulationTwo collaborative 6-axis arms with automatic tool changers
  • SafetyFOD monitoring, fault detection and a hard-wired safe shutdown
  • SitingAdjustable legs level it on unprepared ground
  • PowerSingle-phase mains, with solar for standby
